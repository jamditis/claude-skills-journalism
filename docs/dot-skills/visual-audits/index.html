Visual audits

Look at the whole thing. Then describe what needs fixing well enough that someone can fix it.

When this helps

Use a visual audit before publishing a graphic, shipping an interface, or declaring a design fixed. Source code can tell you what was intended. The rendered result tells you what a reader actually gets.

Give your dot: the exact URL or artifact, revision, intended audience, main task, reference design, screen sizes, and whether you want findings, fixes, or both.

From a vague verdict to a useful finding

Not enough

“Looks good.”

No viewport. No revision. No evidence that the page was opened or the controls were used.

Useful finding

The timestamp is clipped.

At 360 px wide, the tracker’s evidence time runs beyond the card. Let the row wrap, then recheck the same revision at that width and with enlarged text.

Illustrative review notes, not findings about this live page.

The method

  1. Capture the real frame.

    Open the exact revision at desktop and mobile sizes. Keep a full-frame capture before cropping a detail. Record the viewport, browser, and state.

  2. Check the content and composition.

    Look for weak hierarchy, cramped spacing, poor contrast, overflow, unreadable labels, and mismatched numbers. Review a tracker at the size it will appear in chat.

  3. Use the controls.

    Test keyboard focus, links, downloads, repeated clicks, long text, empty states, and failed actions. A screenshot cannot prove an interaction works.

  4. Keep the character. Remove the friction.

    This workshop is intentionally whimsical. Test its bounce, pause control, and reduced-motion mode rather than replacing its personality with the main docs theme.

  5. Recheck the changed result.

    Repeat the original conditions after a fix. Report what is fixed, what remains, and what you could not test.

Give each finding a job

Record the location, observed behavior, evidence, effect on the task, severity, and proposed correction. Mark a design preference as a suggestion, not a defect.

Visual finding severity
SeverityUse it when
BlockingThe primary task cannot be completed or key information is inaccessible.
MajorA substantial usability or accuracy problem has a workaround.
MinorA localized defect does not block the task.
SuggestionYou are proposing an optional improvement or a stylistic preference.

When the browser will not cooperate

Record what failed: the browser did not start, the computer was unreachable, a sign-in expired, or the page returned an error. Those are different problems. Do not claim a reboot fixed access without checking that the computer is reachable again.

Source review and supplied screenshots may still be useful. Label them as code-only or screenshot-only. Neither establishes the current live appearance, and neither justifies “visually verified.”

No amount of careful prompting repairs an unreachable machine. The field notes explain why this is part of the workflow.
