All numbers, events, and times here are examples. This page does not connect to Slack, check a real project, or schedule work.

  1. Define the finish line.

    Choose the deliverables and the proof you need before calculating a percentage. “Code written” and “working for staff” are different milestones.

  2. Keep two clocks.

    A fresh check does not mean fresh progress. A failed check should not make old evidence look current.

  3. Update the same post.

    Prepare the full card, replace the approved original when the tools support it, then read it back. No placeholder post and no surprise extra ping.

Try a recipe.
Keep the receipt.

These are instructions to adapt, not buttons that install access to your accounts. Start small enough that you can check the result.

  1. Read the playbook.

    Choose one job. Review the inputs, examples, and limits before giving the instructions to your dot.

  2. Make the scope concrete.

    Name the destination, posting identity, permitted actions, and what must stay private. Have the dot read back what it actually saved.

  3. Try one low-stakes run.

    Inspect the delivered result. A recurring schedule should come after that check, not stand in for it.

  4. Bring back what happened.

    Share a redacted example, the failure, or an improvement. State what you tested and what you could not test.

A small shelf, with room to grow

Something like
a skill exchange.

I want this to become a place to share useful dot workflows, not just screenshots of things that worked once. For now, it is a small, public collection with readable instructions and visible limits.

A good submission includes a real task, a reusable method, an example, and a frank account of what failed. No one-click installation or blanket compatibility claim is implied.
